> 「Cold Dew is the node of the ninth month. The qi of the dew is cold, and it is about to congeal.」 (「寒露，九月节。露气寒冷，将凝结也。」)
>
> — **Wu Cheng**, ***Monthly Ordinances of the Seventy-Two Pentads*** (《月令七十二候集解》, Yuan dynasty)

## I. What the Term Means: The Dew Turns Cold First, and One Step More Is Frost

**Cold Dew** (*Hanlu*, 寒露) is the seventeenth of the twenty-four solar terms — the nodes by which the traditional Chinese calendar divides the sun's yearly round — and the fifth of autumn. Each year on 8 or 9 October of the Gregorian calendar the sun reaches celestial longitude 195° and the term begins, with the Autumnal Equinox before it and Frost's Descent after. The lunar calendar sets it in the ninth month, and so the *Monthly Ordinances of the Seventy-Two Pentads* names it 「the node of the ninth month」 (「九月节」).

The logic of the name is easy to follow. At White Dew the dew carried a touch of coolness; at Cold Dew the dew already feels cold to the touch — and one step more is frost. The *Classic of Poetry (Book of Odes)*, in "Qi Yue" of the Bin Feng section (《诗经·豳风·七月》), sings the two months in a single line: 「In the ninth month the frost falls stern and clear; in the tenth the threshing floors are swept」 (「九月肃霜，十月涤场。」)

The three pentads — the five-day phases into which each solar term is further divided — write these fifteen days out in fine detail:

| Pentad | Phenomenon | Explanation (after the *Monthly Ordinances of the Seventy-Two Pentads*) |
|--------|------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **First** | **The wild geese arrive as guests** (鸿雁来宾) | 「Of the geese, those that arrived first at mid-autumn are the hosts; those that arrive later, in the last month of autumn, are the guests.」 (「雁以仲秋先至者为主，季秋后至者为宾。」) The geese that left first at White Dew were the masters of the house; this company that brings up the rear counts as the guests. |
| **Second** | **The sparrows enter the great waters and become clams** (雀入大水为蛤) | 「The great waters are the sea... For the cold wind blows stern and severe, and many go into the sea and are transformed into clams — a flying creature become a creature hidden in the waters.」 (「大水，海也……盖寒风严肃，多入于海，变之为蛤，此飞物化为潜物也。」) The small birds have vanished without trace while the clams along the shore are just now at their fattest; the ancients said the birds had turned into the clams. |
| **Third** | **The chrysanthemum puts forth yellow blossoms** (菊有黄华) | 「The grasses and trees all blossom with the yang; the chrysanthemum alone blossoms with the yin... and its color answers exactly to the season of late autumn, when the qi of earth is at its height.」 (「草木皆华于阳，独菊华于阴……其色正应季秋土旺之时也。」) The hundred flowers open in spring's wake; the chrysanthemum alone chooses the days around Cold Dew. |

The chrysanthemum of the third pentad is the very thing the *Book of Rites · Monthly Ordinances* (《礼记·月令》) records: 「In the last month of autumn, the chrysanthemum puts forth yellow blossoms」 (「季秋之月，鞠有黄华」). The term often still catches the Double Ninth Festival (Chongyang) — climbing the heights, admiring the chrysanthemums, chrysanthemum wine. The season's eating has its fixed points too: old Beijing eats sesame at Cold Dew — the Start of Autumn essay has already quoted the *Bencao Gangmu* on sesame's mending the five inner organs and boosting the qi and its strength — and sesame crisps and sesame flatbreads take to the streets at this time; the tea people of Fujian call the autumn tea picked now Cold Dew tea; and the sweet potatoes freshly dug from the fields have come to market — keep them a few days and wait for their sweetness to turn back.

For health, Cold Dew keeps two accounts. One is dryness: the cool dryness of deep autumn bites more sharply than the warm dryness of White Dew, and the throat, the skin, and the bowels are all asking for water. The other is cold: cold comes up from the feet, and cool floors, sandals, and bare feet should all be put away now.

## II. Nourishment: A Spoonful of Pomelo Honey, a Bowl of Sweet-Potato Sweet Soup

Dryness first. The Start of Autumn essay quoted Wu Jutong — 「dryness arises after the Autumnal Equinox... and cool qi takes command」 (「燥气起于秋分以后……凉气司令」) — and by Cold Dew the words have come true: the phlegm turns from yellow and sticky to white and thin, the lips dry until the skin peels, and the throat grows drier still in the night. The old practice of "salt water in the morning, honey water in the evening" fits the moment exactly — mild salt water at morning to soothe the throat, honey at night to moisten the lung and ease the bowels. Pomelo is just in season; make the evening honey into honey pomelo tea, and one jar will hold out half a month.

**Honey pomelo tea**: one pomelo. Break about 400 g of the flesh into small pieces and pick out the seeds; peel the skin, shave away every trace of white pith, cut it into fine shreds — about 100 g — and knead the shreds with a pinch of salt, twice over, rinsing clean each time to draw out the bitterness. Put the flesh, the shreds, 100 g of rock sugar, and about 200 ml of water into a pot and simmer forty minutes over a low fire, stirring along the bottom often, until the syrup thickens and coats the back of the spoon. Take the pot off the fire and let it cool until it no longer scalds the hand, then stir in 150 g of honey and spoon the tea into a scalded glass jar; keep it in the refrigerator. To drink, stir a spoonful — about 15 g — into warm water, never water just off the boil. Those with high blood pressure or weak kidneys should skip that morning half-cup of salt water; those with high blood sugar should take the pomelo honey sparingly; and children under one year of age must not touch honey at all.

But moistening must be paired with warmth. The pomelo honey looks after the mouth, the tongue, and the throat; the chill of deep-autumn morning and evening wants a bowl of hot sweet soup, warming from the stomach outward. The sweet potato is at its newest now: the *Bencao Gangmu* records it as 「ganshu」 (「甘薯」, the sweet potato), where Shizhen writes that it 「supplements deficiency and spent strength, boosts the qi and its strength, strengthens the spleen and stomach, and fortifies the kidney yin」 (「补虚乏，益气力，健脾胃，强肾阴」) — sweet and level in nature, no drastic tonic, but the advance guard for the winter nourishing that begins at Beginning of Winter.

**Sweet-potato and red-date sweet soup**: one sweet potato, about 300 g — peel it and cut it into roll-cut chunks, then rinse off the surface starch in clean water; four red dates (jujubes), broken open and pitted; two small slices of ginger. Put the sweet potato, the dates, and the ginger into about 1,200 ml of cold water; bring to the boil over a high flame, turn the fire low, and cook twenty minutes — until a chopstick slides through the sweet potato with ease and the soup has thickened faintly — then add 20 g of brown sugar and cook five minutes more to melt it, and drink it hot. A sweet potato just out of the ground has not yet turned its starch into sugar; buy it a few days ahead, let it rest, and the sweetness comes thicker. Sweet potato in quantity easily brings gas and acid reflux, so do not spare the two slices of ginger; halve the brown sugar for those with high blood sugar; four or five red dates a day is the measure, fewer still when the belly is already bloated.

## III. Tending What the Season Brings: A Cool Cough, and the Early Morning

Cough first. The rock-sugar stewed pear and the white-fungus soup of the Awakening of Insects essay take the cool-moistening road, made for the dry cough with yellow sticky phlegm and a sore throat; the cough that comes with Cold Dew runs cool — a dry cough, phlegm white and thin, a stopped-up nose, an itchy throat, tightening whenever a cold wind catches it. This is **cool dryness**, and Wu Jutong set out the pattern in full in the "Autumn Dryness" section of the Upper Jiao chapter of the *Wenbing Tiaobian* (《温病条辨》, *Treatise on Warm Diseases*):

> 「When dryness wounds its own organ, the head aches slightly, one shrinks from the cold, the cough brings up thin phlegm, the nose is stopped, the throat feels barred, the pulse is string-taut, and there is no sweat — Xingsu Powder governs it.」 (「燥伤本脏，头微痛，恶寒，咳嗽稀痰，鼻塞，嗌塞，脉弦，无汗，杏苏散主之。」)

Xingsu Powder is the physician's business; at home, three things can be done. While the phlegm is white and thin, do not reach straight for the cool-moistening remedies — Sichuan fritillaria, snow pear, and their like; change the thermos instead to water boiled with one piece of aged tangerine peel and two slices of fresh ginger. Wear a mask going out morning and evening: cold air pouring straight down the throat will only tighten the cough. And if the cough runs past two weeks without stopping, the phlegm turns yellow and thick, or fever rises — go to the hospital.

Then the morning. After Cold Dew, early-morning temperatures in the north fall to around 10 °C, and this hour or two is the one the heart and the blood vessels fear most: when the temperature drops, blood pressure surges easily. Let the morning exercise of the old wait for the sun — do not hurry out at first light. Rise half a beat slower: lie for half a minute after waking, sit up and rest half a minute, then sit on the edge of the bed, legs hanging, half a minute again. Cover head and neck out of doors; take the medicine on time, measure the blood pressure on time. Tightness or pain in the chest, a crooked corner of the mouth, weakness in the limbs of one side — the moment any one of these appears, call 120, China's emergency medical number; do not wait and watch.

## IV. Daily Life: At Cold Dew, Bare Not the Feet

The old saying runs, as the White Dew essay has already quoted it: "At White Dew bare not the body; at Cold Dew bare not the feet." The thin jacket added at morning and evening through White Dew should give way, once Cold Dew has passed, to something thicker. The Start of Autumn essay said autumn freezing must keep its measure — and at Cold Dew that measure begins to draw in, first at the feet: slip into slippers the moment you leave the bed, and do not go barefoot on the cold tiles again; change to thick cotton socks that cover the ankle.

Foot-soaking before bed is this term's most economical health measure: water around 40 °C, deep enough to pass the ankles, fifteen to twenty minutes; stop once the body is gently warm, and do not soak on to a sweat. Wait an hour after eating before you soak. For those with diabetic feet, sensation is dulled — keep the water cooler rather than hotter, and dry the feet well and put on socks as soon as you are done.

Sleep keeps to the way of autumn's three months set out in the *Suwen*'s "Great Treatise on the Four Qi and the Regulation of the Spirit" — retire early, rise early — in bed around half past ten. The term often catches the Double Ninth Festival, and climbing the heights is the month's graceful outing: the gap between the temperature at the foot of the mountain and at the top is wide, so carry a jacket, and when the sweat has come do not strip off your clothes and stand in the wind. Deep autumn is the season when spirits easily sink; to climb high and look far is itself the remedy — and you may admire the chrysanthemums on the way.

## V. Three Foods of the Season

**Chrysanthemum**. The third pentad — 「the chrysanthemum puts forth yellow blossoms」 (「菊有黄华」) — is the chrysanthemum's own season. The *Shennong Bencao Jing* (《神农本草经》, *The Divine Farmer's Classic of Materia Medica*) ranks it among the upper grade: 「It governs the various winds and dizziness of the head... Long-continued use frees the blood and qi, lightens the body, holds off age, and lengthens the years.」 (「主诸风，头眩……久服利血气，轻身耐老延年。」) Day to day, take three to five blooms of Hangzhou white chrysanthemum, cover with boiling water, and let it stand two or three minutes; add a dozen or so goji berries — one cup in the afternoon for dry eyes and a full, tight head. Those with a cold stomach should not drink it strong, and not on an empty stomach.

**Corn**. New corn comes to market now, and its sweetness old corn cannot match. Steam the whole cobs twenty minutes and eat one with a bowl of congee for breakfast; northerners simmer cracked corn into a grits porridge, best when a thin film of oil gathers on its surface. When you boil corn, leave the silk in the pot to cook along with it, and drink that water as tea — folk usage takes it to help the body pass water.

**Taro**. Deep-autumn taro is floury and melting: dice Lipu taro — the great fragrant taro of Lipu in Guangxi — and cook it with rice into a pot of rice, so fragrant the lid comes off early; or steam the small taro corms whole in their skins and dip them in soy sauce, standing in for half a small bowl of rice. The slime of raw taro makes the hands itch: slip a plastic bag over your hands when peeling, and if the itch comes all the same, rubbing with ginger juice will ease it. Raw taro must never be eaten.
