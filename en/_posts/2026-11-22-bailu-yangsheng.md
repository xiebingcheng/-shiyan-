> 「The reeds are lush and green; the white dew turns to frost; the one of whom I speak is on the other side of the water.」 (「蒹葭苍苍，白露为霜。所谓伊人，在水一方。」)
>
> — ***Classic of Poetry (Book of Odes)***, "Jian Jia" of the Qin Feng section (《诗经·秦风·蒹葭》)

## I. What the Term Means: Dew Congeals to White, and Each Night Is Cooler Than the Last

**White Dew** (*Bailu*, 白露) is the fifteenth of the twenty-four solar terms — the nodes by which the traditional Chinese calendar divides the sun's yearly round — and the third of autumn. Each year on 7 or 8 September of the Gregorian calendar the sun reaches celestial longitude 165° and the term begins, with Start of Autumn and End of Heat before it and the Autumn Equinox after.

The Start of Autumn essay quoted the second pentad of the *Monthly Ordinances of the Seventy-Two Pentads*, 「White Dew Descends」 (「白露降」): then the dew had only 「descended」 (「降」), the herald of coming cool. By White Dew the yin qi has grown heavier, and the dew truly congeals white. Read straight through, the three pentads — the five-day phases into which each solar term is further divided — are all the stirrings of packing up:

| Pentad | Phenomenon | Explanation (after the *Monthly Ordinances of the Seventy-Two Pentads*) |
|--------|------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **First** | **The wild geese come** (鸿雁来) | 「The *hong* is the larger and the *yan* the smaller of the wild geese; they come from the north and make their way south. It is not said that they head for the south, for the south is not where they dwell.」 (「鸿大雁小，自北而来南也。不谓南乡，非其居耳。」) |
| **Second** | **The swallows return** (玄鸟归) | 「At this time they go from the south toward the north. The swallow is a bird of the north, and so it is said to be 'returning.'」 (「此时自南而往北也。燕乃北方之鸟，故曰归。」) |
| **Third** | **The birds store up provisions** (群鸟养羞) | 「*Xiu* is the food that is simmered into broth; to store provisions means to lay it by against the nourishment the winter months will ask.」 (「羞者，所羹之食。养羞者，藏之以备冬月之养也。」) |

Geese and swallows set out from both ends of the sky, and even the birds have begun laying in winter stores. For people, the summer heat at last leaves the stage: the "autumn tiger" (*qiu laohu*) — the fierce late-summer heat that in the Start of Autumn essay had yet to go down the mountain — has vanished without a trace by White Dew. The folk proverb runs, "Nights of White Dew and the Autumnal Equinox — each night colder than the last." The daytime may still hold 27 or 28 °C, while the morning and evening are left with only 17 or 18 °C; a single day passes through two seasons. Du Fu took this for the opening of his "Moonlit Night, Remembering My Brother": 「The dew turns white from this night on; the moon is brightest in my old home.」 (「露从今夜白，月是故乡明。」)

The day keeps its local appointments across the country: old Nanjing drinks its White Dew tea; Fuzhou holds that no White Dew may pass without longan; Zixing in Hunan brews White Dew rice wine; Wenzhou eats sweet potato; and in Cangnan, Zhejiang, people gather the "Ten Whites" (*shi yang bai*) — ten herbs whose names all carry the word "white" — and stew them with black-bone chicken. The customs go by different names, but the reasoning is one: it has turned cool, and the body must be led gently over toward autumn and winter.

## II. Nourishment: A Pot of White Dew Tea, a Bowl of Longan-Boiled Egg

Tea first. The folk proverb runs, "Spring tea is bitter, summer tea astringent; when you want tea worth drinking, autumn's White Dew." The tea bush has weathered a whole summer, and the autumn tea it puts out around White Dew — the autumn flush — comes full of fragrance and mellow of taste, and stands up to steeping after steeping; this is exactly the cup the old households of Nanjing cherish. The brewing runs exactly opposite to the Mingqian tea of the Pure Brightness essay: that first-picking leaf is tender and takes water at 85 °C, poured and drunk at once; autumn tea wants hotter water to give up its flavor. In a cup, take 4 g of dry leaf and water at over 90 °C; pour off the first steep after ten seconds or so, and do not let it sit. In a gaiwan — the lidded bowl of Chinese tea-making — take 5 g, boiling water in and out quickly, and the leaf will carry three or four steepings. Drink it in the morning, and keep strong tea off an empty stomach.

Then the longan. In Fuzhou, no White Dew may pass without longan, and the saying grows so grand as to claim "one longan is worth a chicken" — take the lucky flourish for a flourish. Li Shizhen, in his *Bencao Gangmu* (《本草纲目》, *Compendium of Materia Medica*), set it beside the lychee: 「As food the lychee is prized, but for nourishment the longan is better」 (「食品以荔枝为贵，而资益则龙眼为良」) — the lychee runs hot in nature, the longan mild and even, and nourishment need not be rushed into a single fruit.

**Longan-boiled egg**: 15 g of dried longan flesh (about 30 g of fresh longans, shelled and pitted), one egg, and 10 g of brown sugar, which may be left out. Add 400 ml of water to the dried longan flesh and simmer fifteen minutes over a low fire, until the sweetness is fragrant; crack in the egg and let it poach whole, three or four minutes more, and take it off the heat just as the yolk is on the point of setting — eat it while warm. Take it at breakfast, alongside a staple food, two or three mornings a week. Longan is warming after all: keep dried longan flesh to about 10 g a day. If you are running inner heat just now — a sore throat, a thick yellow tongue coating, acne that keeps coming — set it aside for a few days; those with high blood sugar should leave out the sugar and halve the longan.

Zixing's White Dew rice wine is an old custom, warming to the middle and sweet. But wine is wine: one cup is the limit, and those who will drive should not touch it; for those who keep off alcohol, a bowl of sweet fermented rice with little glutinous dumplings is quite enough. As for autumn dryness, the lesson began back with the End of Heat essay — the rock-sugar stewed pear and the white-fungus soup of the Awakening of Insects essay still do their work; turn back and follow them as written, and White Dew needs no new stove of its own. The Start of Autumn essay said it too: sticking on autumn fat is folk custom, not medical advice. By White Dew there is no more fat to stick — step the table one pace toward the gently stewed and the plainly light, and that is enough.

## III. Tending What the Season Brings: The Cough of Cool Dryness, the Diarrhea of Stored-Up Chill

After White Dew, a cough mostly carries the word "cool": a dry cough, phlegm white and thin, an itchy throat without redness or swelling, a blocked nose, a slight chilliness. This is **cool dryness** — the counterpart of warm dryness — and Wu Jutong's Xingsu Powder, quoted in the End of Heat essay, treats precisely this line. What the home can offer is warm moistening: 2–3 g of aged tangerine peel with two slices of fresh ginger, boiled in water and taken as tea — drink it warm, in small mouthfuls, swallowed slowly. Do not be quick to pour in the rock-sugar stewed pear and keep it coming: feed cool dryness more cold, and the cough will only drag on longer. The pear's stage is warm dryness — the line of Sangxing Decoction in the Start of Autumn essay: a red and swollen sore throat, yellow sticky phlegm, thirst — only then does the pear take the floor.

Diarrhea is the other end. The account run up by summer's fondness for cold — iced drinks, chilled melon, air conditioning blown straight at the belly, cold dishes left overnight — begins to be settled after White Dew; every item is on the bill. The Start of Autumn essay taught "do not eat melons in autumn"; by White Dew it should be enforced in earnest, and watermelon and such cold melons and fruits are all but closed off. Keep the navel covered, keep the belly covered in sleep — that does more good than any folk remedy. If it truly comes, eat plainly for two days first: millet congee, soft noodles, water made up in small amounts many times over. Vomiting along with the diarrhea, fever, or blood in the stool — do not tough it out; go to the hospital.

## IV. Daily Life: Bare Not the Body at White Dew, and Autumn Freezing Has Its Measure

The old saying runs: "At White Dew bare not the body; at Cold Dew bare not the feet." Bare not the body means the limbs stay covered in the early morning and evening: carry a thin jacket or a vest with you and put it on morning and night; the navel, the ankles, and the shoulders and neck — these three are not to be left exposed, which is just the scope of "freezing" the End of Heat essay mapped out. Morning runners, cyclists, and anyone still out after ten at night: add a layer first, and do not wait to be chilled through before making it up.

**Autumn freezing** (*qiu dong* — the autumn-side counterpart of spring covering, letting the body meet the cool by degrees) is still within its window — the End of Heat essay said that after Frost's Descent one should freeze no longer — but the freezing is done by day, letting the body grow slowly used to the cool, not gritting through until the shivers come. The old, the young, and anyone with old ailments of the heart, the blood vessels, or the joints stop freezing from White Dew on and go straight by "bare not the body." The temperature signal may be turned around from the threshold of the Grain Rain essay: in spring, when the daily low has held steadily above 15 °C, spring covering closes its stall; in autumn the sign runs in reverse — once the daily low falls below 15 °C, the jacket does not leave your side.

Sleep keeps to the Start of Autumn essay's line from the *Suwen*: 「retire early and rise early, with the roosters」 (「早卧早起，与鸡俱兴」). The nights lengthen day by day; be in bed around half past ten. The dawn light comes later too — do not make sleepiness the excuse for staying burrowed in the covers; if rising is hard, begin by drawing the curtain open a crack.

## V. Three Foods of the Season

**Pumpkin**. Autumn pumpkin is floury and sweet. The *Bencao Gangmu*, in its "Vegetables" section (菜部), records it as 「sweet, warm, and without toxicity」 (「甘，温，无毒」), with Shizhen's warning that 「eaten in excess it gives rise to leg-qi and jaundice」 (「多食发脚气、黄疸」) — even a good thing must not stand in for rice. Peel it, cut it into thick chunks, and steam twenty minutes over boiling water to eat as a staple; or simmer 200 g of chunks with 50 g of rice as congee. Do not take the old claim that "pumpkin lowers blood sugar" at face value: the right way is to count it among the staples and let it replace half a bowl of rice; adding it freely as a vegetable runs exactly against the point.

**Lotus seeds**. The *Shennong Bencao Jing* (《神农本草经》, *The Divine Farmer's Classic of Materia Medica*) ranks it among the upper grade, under its old name 「the fruiting stem of the lotus root」 (「藕实茎」): 「It governs the supplementing of the center and the nourishing of the spirit, boosting the qi and its strength and clearing away the hundred ailments. Long-continued use lightens the body and holds off age, stays hunger, and lengthens the years.」 (「主补中养神，益气力，除百疾。久服轻身耐老，不饥延年。」) Soak 20 g of dried lotus seeds an hour ahead and cook them with rice into congee — or drop them into the longan-boiled egg to cook along. Do not cook lotus seeds in an iron pot, or the soup turns black; change to a clay pot or enamelware. Cooked with the green core left in, they lean bitter and cooling; if bitterness displeases, push the core out first.

**Apples**. Around White Dew the early varieties come to market one after another, and eaten raw they are the simplest thing of all. For spleens and stomachs that fear the cold: core one apple, cut it into chunks, simmer ten minutes in 600 ml of water, let it cool to warm, and eat fruit and liquid together. With "do not eat melons in autumn" now closed for the season, when the sweet tooth speaks, this is the very thing to take melon's place. One apple a day is the limit for those with high blood sugar.
